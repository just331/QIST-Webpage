# Evidence-based Materials for Teaching Quantum Information Science and Technology
## Overview
Recent growth in Quantum Information Science and Technology (QIST) in both the governmental and commercial sectors is opening up jobs for quantum computer scientists and challenging universities to build interdisciplinary STEM pathways to degrees in QIST. We are researching students’ strengths and difficulties in understanding and applying Quantum Computing (QC) concepts and skills learned in class and developing evidence-based materials for use in undergraduate QIST courses. The main goals of this project are to:

* Answer the research question: What are students’ strengths and difficulties in an upper-level introductory undergraduate QIST course?
* Develop a set of evidence-based materials (pre- and post-tests and mini-tutorials) to be used in an upper-level introductory undergraduate QIST course. 

Based on our research data, we are identifying common strengths and difficulties with QIST concepts and reasoning skills and then developing sets of pre-and post-tests and mini-tutorials to help educate undergraduate students studying QIST. The mini-tutorials are designed to be done in class in 20-30 minutes and focus on enhancing students’ ability to apply important concepts and skills already introduced in class. Each mini-tutorial has a particular purpose. They may, for example, ask students to use different methods or notations to answer a question. Or they may be used to “discover” results or processes not covered in class. The mini-tutorials on higher level protocols and algorithms often use near transfer as a pedagogy, having students work through a protocol or algorithm with a different starting condition than explored in class or in the text. 

We are pre- and post-testing the students in class before and after working through the mini-tutorials and videotaping the students while they are working through the mini-tutorials. We have developed rubrics for coding the pre- and post-tests and we are also applying a coding scheme to the in-class videos. On this website we present samples of our mini-tutorials, pre- and post-tests, and coding rubrics.

We are an interdisciplinary collaboration, consisting of researchers and educators in QIST across physics, computer science and science education. If you are interested in using or adapting our materials, you may contact us at one of the email addresses below.
